A two-colour screen-print tee. The back carries the weight: a field of binary in which the lit digits spell the letters themselves — 01001101 for M, 01001001 for I. The front stays quiet, one chest lockup, so the back reads as the statement.

How the grid reads

ASCII, 8 bits per letter
Byte 01 — decimal 77
M

Every lit digit in the back grid belongs to this byte, repeating across the letterform. The unlit field is filler noise, printed at 5–20% and cleared entirely for a cell or two around each letter, so the shapes carry at a distance.

Byte 02 — decimal 73
I

The same two bytes are set as plain type under the main lockup and again on the sleeve, so the joke lands even for someone who never decodes the grid.

Filler digits sit at 5–20% opacity. Screen printing cannot hold that as a solid, so it prints as a halftone — ask for a fine mesh (120T+) and a 55 lpi halftone, or have the printer knock the four opacity tiers back to flat tints. DTG or DTF holds the tiers as-is with no extra setup and is the safer route for a short run.
